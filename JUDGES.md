# VeriFlow in one page

**When one AI agent pays another, it cannot tell who it is paying, the seller cannot tell who is buying, and nobody can prove what was sold wasn't changed afterwards. VeriFlow closes all three with Concordium, and every claim on this page is a transaction you can open.**

- **Any x402 agent can check the seller before it signs.** `protect(client)` wraps the `@x402/fetch` client an agent already has: a standalone package on npm, [`concordium-x402-guard`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/concordium-x402-guard) (`npm install concordium-x402-guard`, [source](packages/concordium-x402-guard)), testnet and mainnet. It reads the seller from the Concordium Agent Registry and pays only if the payout address is that agent's own on-chain wallet, or, for a USDC payout on Base, a key bound to the agent's owner in CIS-8. A copycat seller with a stolen card and badge is refused before any signature. **We ran it on seven sellers we do not run** ([below](#the-guard-on-sellers-we-do-not-run)): it passed MainsMarker (#990, another team) and bought from it, passed Oracle Scout (#1253, another team) and bought from it, passed x402 Doctor (#5120) **on mainnet** including its Base payout, and refused four: vet402 and its two test sellers (payout address not the registered wallet; one of them takes payment and returns nothing) and Clear to Pay (no Agent Card on the site).
- **The seller checks the buyer before settling.** A zero-knowledge proof (18+, not in a restricted country, nothing else revealed) is re-checked at every payment, and the payer must be the wallet of the agent it claims to be. Any x402 seller can add the same checks to its own server: [`concordium-x402-seller`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/concordium-x402-seller) ([source](packages/concordium-x402-seller)) attaches them to an `x402ResourceServer` in one call, plus the on-chain recovery of settlements a facilitator misreports and once-only delivery.
- **The chain can enforce the payout too.** Our escrow contract [`<13171,0>`](https://ccdexplorer.io/testnet/contract/13171/0) reads the Agent Registry itself, in the same transaction: a payment to an agent that does not exist, is Revoked, or has no wallet is a rejected transaction, and a payout can only reach the `agentWallet` recorded at payment, and only while the registry still names it. **Try it on the live site, no wallet: "Pay #1313 through the escrow".** Its module is a verifiable build: CCDExplorer shows it **Verified** ("Source and module match"), source included.
- **What was sold cannot be rewritten.** Each call is committed on-chain before its outcome, one per asset per day, under a scoring rule registered before the first commit.

Our own seller is the reference for the last two: it sells a BTC or ETH call, `RISK_ON` or `RISK_OFF`, from Binance's 30-day momentum, for 1 testnet CCD over x402, or 1 `EUDemo` (Concordium's allow-listed test euro, a Protocol-Level Token), or 1 `hh-VFUSD` (a PLT test dollar we issued ourselves), or 0.01 USDC on Base. The rule is deliberately simple and public. The product is the checks around it.

## Built to fail closed

Tests, run in CI on every push (the contract's whenever it changes): 179 for the gateway (TypeScript), 29 for the guard package, 40 for the seller package, 25 for the Python API and verifier, 10 for the v2 contract and 14 for the escrow contract (Rust).

- A node error, an unreachable registry, a changed card or a revoked binding stops the payment before it settles.
- The buyer's checks run in an x402 client hook, so a failing seller is never signed for.
- When a facilitator reports a failure for a transfer that did go through, the gateway reads the transaction itself (CCD and PLT transfers from a node, USDC from the Base receipt) and delivers only on an exact match of payer, `payTo`, token and amount.
- Outside reviews, and what they changed. Escrow v1 → v2 (payout only to the wallet recorded at payment, nothing can stay stuck), shown on-chain above. The two npm packages, 2026-11-27, read line by line against `@x402/core` 2.32: the guard now pays only the `exact` scheme (an EVM `auth-capture` 402 could have sent the whole amount to a fee recipient while `payTo` passed every check); the seller holds each signed payment, by sender and nonce, from verify to the end of settlement, so parallel copies cannot each run the handler; with buyer checks on, a payment offered on another network is refused rather than unchecked; a recovery is not made while the payment ledger is unavailable. Guard 0.2.3, seller 0.1.2, and the gateway the same. Not changed, and why: a copied badge on an impostor site still routes the money only to the real agent's registered wallet.

## Hard questions, answered from the record

**Why not just use Concordium's badge check?** The badge says an agent is registered and its owner verified. It says nothing about the address in a 402's `payTo`: an impostor can serve a real agent's badge and its own wallet. The guard runs the official badge check as one of its five checks and adds the one the badge cannot make, `payTo` = the agent's on-chain `agentWallet` read from a node. vet402 (#1268) and its S2 and S3 above pass the badge and the official check, and are refused on exactly that.

**How is this different from x402 Doctor?** Doctor also checks a seller before paying, as a hosted preflight service (each check is itself a small paid call) with a client that skips a no-go, and it is on Concordium mainnet. VeriFlow's guard reads the seller straight from a Concordium node inside the paying agent, with no hosted service to trust or pay, and fails closed. Around it VeriFlow adds what Doctor does not: the seller side (the buyer's zero-knowledge proof and agent, re-checked at every payment and recorded on-chain per payment), an escrow contract that reads the registry itself and pays only the wallet recorded at payment, and calls committed on-chain before their outcome. They are complementary: our guard checks Doctor's mainnet agent #5120 live (site, Try 3) and it passes.

**Why charge for a call with no score?** We do not sell an edge, and claim none. The call is a deliberately simple public rule, the reference product that every check runs around. What a buyer gets is a call fixed on-chain before its outcome under a rule registered first. The first reveals are due 29 Nov and the first score 30 Nov; until then the honest number is "none yet".

**One friend is not real use.** Agreed. Outside the team: two buyer accounts (19760 from the hackathon Discord, 19836 a friend new to Concordium, who also found and reported a real Windows bug), and our guard bought from two other teams' agents. We do not claim demand; the npm packages are what another team would adopt.

**Does anyone outside the team build on your formats?** In the x402 repository, the author of the Concordium identity-extension proposal, [issue #4182](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/issues/4182), replied to our implementation on 2026-11-22: "good to see extra.buyerProof work in a second implementation", and called our point on binding the payee from the 402 itself fair. The thread has since moved the requirement to one top-level `extensions` entry covering every `accepts` entry, with a restructured proposal promised for the second week of December. Our 402 carries both shapes now (`extra.buyerProof` and `extensions["buyer-proof"]`) and will follow the final field names. This is a discussion, not an adopted standard.

**Contract v2 shows "Unverifiable" on CCDExplorer; why trust it?** It was built before we used Concordium's verifiable-build image, so CCDExplorer cannot match it to source. Anyone can still: CI rebuilds `contract-v2/` on every change and fails unless the result is exactly the deployed module `08e3b49e…` (`.github/workflows/contract.yml`). The escrow, built later, is Verified on CCDExplorer. The mainnet v2 will be a verifiable build ([GO_LIVE.md](docs/GO_LIVE.md), step 9).

**Two transactions on 22 Nov around the scoring rule?** One rule. [`ecadec1b…`](https://ccdexplorer.io/testnet/transaction/ecadec1b75cd764a2f87c512787cb3e5422148852760046978e2e4324d8b3271) registers it; [`6e3eb548…`](https://ccdexplorer.io/testnet/transaction/6e3eb5483acbcdbf87717649b0941812513220c52b0623cc652c80678dc7668f), 27 minutes later, is `set_committer`, which gave the contract its committer. No call could be committed under v2 before the rule was on-chain.

**Can the seller skip bad days?** No. The rule requires a commit for every asset and day in the window, and "no commit" is a failure; one failed slot blocks the whole number ([SCORING-v2.md](docs/SCORING-v2.md), "When it is published"). Skipping a day does not hide a loss, it stops the score from being published at all.

**The committer writes `momentum_e6` itself. Who checks it?** The scoring rule recomputes it from Binance's daily closes and counts any difference as a failure. The contract also checks that the label matches its sign at reveal.

**A stored proof, re-checked: what if the credential changes?** At each payment the seller re-runs the SDK's official check on the stored proof, reads the identity provider list from a node, requires both anchors to be its own, and requires the credential to still belong to the paying account. Since 27 Nov each payment's re-check is also recorded on-chain (for example [`e630b294…`](https://ccdexplorer.io/testnet/transaction/e630b2945f514ba6960ba759760e4f4cee43fcdf55814127c36bfc0d36c73d39)). A new proof is required after 30 days.

**Concordium's x402 guide checks attributes at the facilitator. Why in the seller?** So the policy belongs to the seller and survives a change of facilitator, and the seller does not have to trust the facilitator with it. The same code runs with Concordium's hosted facilitator and with our own.

**Fail closed means no payment when the registry service is down.** Yes, by design: a payment that cannot be checked is not made. The guard reads the agent from a node; the hosted service is used for the official badge check, and its answer must agree with the node. An outage costs a sale, never money sent to the wrong place.

**322 mainnet cards mention x402 and one endpoint is live. Who is protected?** Few today; the census says so. The checks are cheap to add before agents pay each other at volume, which is why they ship as packages rather than as a service to call.

**Who pays the fees at scale?** On testnet Concordium's hosted facilitator sponsors buyers' fees. With our own facilitator each sale costs us about Ͼ5 in sponsored fees (testnet showed Ͼ5.0–5.5 per transaction), so a mainnet price must sit above that; the running cost of the daily record is about Ͼ15,000 a year ([GO_LIVE.md](docs/GO_LIVE.md#costs)). The guard, the seller package and the escrow charge no fee.

## What it does not claim

- The signal is a public 30-day momentum rule with no edge. The product is the accountability around it.
- Everything that sends a transaction (payments, escrow, commits, proof anchors) runs on testnet with testnet identity providers. Against mainnet we only read: the published guard checks live agents such as x402 Doctor (#5120), and the census read the whole mainnet registry. We have no mainnet agent, contract or payment yet.
- No call has been revealed or scored yet (first reveal 2026-11-29).
- Most purchases are our own agent's (18 of 20 as of 2026-11-26); the other two are a friend of the developer and a tester from the hackathon's Discord.
